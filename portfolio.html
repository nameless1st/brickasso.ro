Artistic practice

Brickasso® uses LEGO® elements as material, while each artwork begins with an idea, a composition and a story that continues beyond the visible image.

Albert develops his compositions without automated image-to-mosaic conversion software. He selects and places the elements by hand, deciding how colour, volume and depth work together across the surface. Planning, testing and revision continue throughout construction.

From a distance, the viewer reads the main image. Up close, miniature scenes reveal another story: a life, a memory, a historical moment or a personal connection. The composition is developed to hold these different readings within one physical artwork.

Artistic characteristics

The following characteristics describe the Brickasso® body of work through facts that can be observed, documented and verified.

One of one originals

Each registered Brickasso® original remains a unique physical artwork. Selected artworks may also be released separately as signed and numbered Fine Art editions, without changing the original work's BRK Registry identity.

Hand built

Each work develops through concept, material selection, sorting, construction tests and revision. Major works can require hundreds of hours of manual construction, with additional time devoted to research, sourcing and documentation.

Hidden narratives

Miniature scenes are composed around the subject of the work. Memories, biographical details and historical references become part of the larger image, connecting close viewing with the story seen from a distance.

Sculptural relief

Layered construction creates physical volume, changing shadows and different views of the same subject. Selected works introduce movement, making mechanical construction part of the artistic experience.

Commissions

Selected commissions are considered when the subject, scale and story can become part of the artistic direction of Brickasso®.

Each commission remains a one of one artwork. The process includes concept, visual direction, hidden narrative, construction time, framing, transport planning and certificate documentation.

Each proposal is considered individually, including the subject, research, scale, narrative scenes and any sculptural or moving elements. Timing and acquisition terms are provided for the specific work. Major commissions may require several months from concept to completion.

Frequently asked questions

These answers explain how Brickasso® works are documented, created and placed within the official catalogue.

Are the compositions generated automatically?

No. Albert develops the compositions without automated image-to-mosaic conversion software. Colour, form, relief and the placement of miniature scenes are developed through his own decisions and manual construction.

What goes into creating a Brickasso® original?

The process can include research, concept development, composition, sourcing and sorting elements, construction tests, revisions and hundreds of hours of manual work. Narrative scenes and any structural or moving elements are developed for the individual artwork, followed by finishing and documentation.

How is the price of an original artwork determined?

Each original artwork and private commission is considered individually, taking into account its artistic concept, scale, narrative development, sculptural and technical complexity, and documented history. The number of LEGO® elements or construction hours alone does not determine its price. Current acquisition terms are available directly from Brickasso management for the specific work. Fine Art print editions are separate from the original physical artworks.

Are Brickasso® artworks reproduced as editions?

Registered Brickasso® originals remain unique one of one physical artworks. Selected artworks may also be released as separately documented, signed and numbered Fine Art editions. The original physical artwork retains its individual BRK Registry identity.

How is authenticity verified?

Each official work receives a BRK number and is accompanied by signed documentation. QR verification connects the certificate to the official Brickasso® website.

How many major works can be created each year?

The number depends on size and complexity. Monumental works can require hundreds of hours and several months of manual construction, which naturally limits annual production.

How are commissions considered?

Selected commissions are considered when the subject, scale and story match the artistic direction of Brickasso®. Each commission remains a one of one artwork.

Where is the complete list of works?

The complete chronology is documented in the official Brickasso® Catalogue, where each registered artwork appears by BRK number.
